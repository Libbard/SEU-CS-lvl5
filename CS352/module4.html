Determining System Requirements

"Garbage In, Garbage Out." The success of a system depends entirely on how well we gather, analyze, and structure the requirements.

The Requirements Process

The Goal

We gather information on what the system should do from users, reports, forms, and procedures. This information is then transformed into a structured format.

Requirements Determination Gathering
Requirements Structuring Modeling

Deliverables

  • Information Collected: Interview transcripts, observation notes, meeting minutes .
  • Understanding: Business mission, objectives, and information processing needs.

Traditional Methods

1. Interviewing

The primary way analysts gather information. Can be Individual or Group .

Open-Ended Questions

"What do you think about...?"

PRO: Breadth of detail. CON: Hard to analyze .

Closed-Ended Questions

"Do you use option A or B?"

PRO: Easy to analyze. CON: Limited info .

2. Direct Observation

Watching users work. Validates what they *say* vs what they *do*.
Risk: Hawthorne Effect (people behave differently when watched) .

3. Document Analysis

Reviewing forms, reports, and manuals. Helps understand the "Formal System" vs the "Informal System" and find gaps .

Modern Methods

JAD (Joint Application Design)

Intensive group-oriented requirements determination technique. Team members meet in isolation for an extended period.

Session Leader: Facilitates, neutral.
Scribe: Takes notes (CASE tools).
Users/Managers: Active participants.

Goal: Resolve conflicts fast & build consensus.

Prototyping

Iterative process of building a rudimentary version of the system.

  • Evolutionary: Prototype becomes the final system.
  • Throwaway: Prototype helps define reqs, then discarded.
Best when: Requirements are not well understood or communication problems exist .

Radical Methods

BPR (Business Process Reengineering)

The search for, and implementation of, radical change in business processes to achieve breakthrough improvements.

"Obliterate, don't automate."

Professor's Secrets & Trap Avoidance

TRAP: Interviewing vs JAD

Interviewing is time-consuming and can lead to conflicting requirements (one person says X, another says Y). JAD solves conflicts *during* the session but is expensive (key people off-site for days). Know the trade-off.

TRAP: Prototyping Drawbacks

Prototyping is great, but it has a hidden trap: Skipping Validation. Users might see a working prototype and assume the system is "done," ignoring reliability, security, and performance checks .

SECRET: The Hawthorne Effect

When observing users, they often perform better or differently simply *because they are being watched*. This biases your data. Always mention this limitation when discussing Observation.

KEY CONCEPT: Formal vs Informal Systems

Formal: What the procedure manual says (Document Analysis). Informal: How people actually do the work (Observation). The gap between these two is where requirements usually hide.
