1. Identifying & Selecting Projects

Sources of Potential Projects

Top-Down (Strategic)
  • Top Management: Greater strategic focus, largest size/duration.
  • Steering Committee: Cross-functional focus, enterprise-wide consideration.
Bottom-Up (Operational)
  • User Departments: Narrow, non-strategic focus. Faster development.
  • Development Group: Integration with existing systems focus.

Evaluation Criteria

Projects are ranked based on their relative merit. Key criteria include:

  • Value Chain Analysis: Does this activity add value or just cost? Comparison with competitors .
  • Strategic Alignment: Does it help achieve long-term goals?.
  • Resource Availability: Do we have the people, time, and money?.

2. Corporate Strategy

Effective IS planning must align with the broader corporate strategy. It is a three-step process: Current Enterprise $\to$ Future Enterprise $\to$ Strategic Plan .

Key Definitions

  • Mission Statement: What business are we in? (e.g., "Selling high-quality wood furniture") .
  • Objective Statements: Qualitative/Quantitative goals (e.g., "Increase market share").

Generic Competitive Strategies

Low-Cost Producer Competing on price (e.g., Hyundai).
Product Differentiation Competing on quality/style (e.g., Ford).
Product Niche Narrow market focus (e.g., Convertibles).

3. Information Systems Planning (ISP)

The ISP Process

  1. Assess Current Situation: Inventory of processes, data, and technology.
  2. Define Future Situation: Blueprints of manual/automated processes and data.
  3. Develop Schedule: Create a roadmap of projects to transition from Current to Future.

Planning Matrices

Matrices map relationships between organizational elements to identify gaps and redundancies .

Location-to-Function: Where functions are performed.
Function-to-Process: Which processes support functions.
Data Entity-to-System: CRUD analysis (Create, Read, Update, Delete).
System-to-Objective: Alignment check.

Professor's Secrets & Trap Avoidance

TRAP: Top-Down vs Bottom-Up

Top-Down: Strategic, broader perspective, better integration, but slower. Bottom-Up: Tactical, faster development, solves specific problems, but may lead to redundant/incompatible systems.
Don't confuse "Development Group" (Bottom-up) with "Steering Committee" (Top-down).

TRAP: Incremental Commitment

The project is NOT fully approved at the start. It is reviewed after each phase. Just because you finish Analysis doesn't mean you automatically start Design; the project must be re-justified.

SECRET: Value Chain Analysis

The purpose of Value Chain Analysis is to identify where an organization adds value to its products/services and where costs are incurred. It helps rank projects by asking: "Does this project optimize a high-value activity?".

KEY CONCEPT: Matrices

Matrices (like Process-to-Data) are the glue between Business Planning and IS Planning. They detect:
1. Missing systems (Function not supported).
2. Redundant data (Data created in multiple places).
