Advanced ER: Enhanced ER (EER)

Extending the ER model with Object-Oriented concepts: Subclasses, Superclasses, Inheritance, and Specialization.

Ternary Relationships Subclass/Superclass Inheritance Specialization Generalization

Higher-Degree Relationships

While binary relationships (degree 2) are most common, some situations require degree > 2.

Ternary Relationship (Degree 3)

Example: SUPPLY relates Supplier, Part, and Project simultaneously.

  • Cannot be easily decomposed into 3 binary relationships without losing semantics.
  • Attributes (e.g., Quantity) belong to the combination of all three.
Aggregation

An abstraction concept for building composite objects. Used when a relationship itself needs to participate in another relationship.

Subclasses & Superclasses

The Concept

An entity type may have meaningful subgroupings.
Example: EMPLOYEE may be subdivided into SECRETARY, ENGINEER, TECHNICIAN.

  • Superclass: The generic entity (EMPLOYEE).
  • Subclass: The specific subgroup (ENGINEER).
  • Relationship: IS-A (e.g., ENGINEER IS-A EMPLOYEE).

Attribute Inheritance

An entity in the Subclass inherits:

  1. All Attributes of the Superclass.
  2. All Relationships the Superclass participates in.
Local Attributes: Attributes specific to the subclass (e.g., `TypingSpeed` for SECRETARY).

Specialization vs Generalization

Specialization

Top-Down Process

Start with a single entity (EMPLOYEE) and define subclasses (SECRETARY, ENGINEER) based on distinguishing characteristics.

Generalization

Bottom-Up Process

Start with separate entities (CAR, TRUCK) and generalize them into a single superclass (VEHICLE) based on common features.

Constraints

1. Disjointness Constraint

Can an entity be a member of more than one subclass?

  • d Disjoint: NO overlap. (Entity must be either A or B, not both).
  • o Overlapping: Overlap allowed. (Entity can be both A and B).

2. Completeness Constraint

Must every entity in the superclass be a member of some subclass?

  • Total YES. (Double Line). Every Employee MUST be a Sec, Eng, or Tech.
  • Partial NO. (Single Line). An Employee can exist without being any of the subclasses.

Professor's Secrets & Trap Avoidance

TRAP: Disjoint vs Overlapping

Disjoint (d): E.g., Employee is either Salaried OR Hourly. Cannot be both.
Overlapping (o): E.g., Person can be Employee AND Student. Look for the little 'd' or 'o' inside the circle in EER diagrams.

TRAP: Inheritance Direction

Attributes flow DOWN. Subclasses inherit from Superclass. Subclasses NEVER pass their specific attributes UP to the Superclass.

SECRET: Union Type

A subclass with multiple superclasses is a Shared Subclass (Multiple Inheritance). If the superclasses are different entity types (e.g., Car and Truck owning a "RegisteredVehicle" license), it's called a Category (Union Type).

KEY CONCEPT: When to Specialize?

Create a subclass only if:
1. The subclass has unique attributes not shared by others.
2. The subclass participates in a unique relationship.
