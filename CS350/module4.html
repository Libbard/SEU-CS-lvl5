Entities & Attributes

Entity Type vs Set

  • Entity: A specific object in the real world (e.g., Employee "John").
  • Entity Type: Defines a collection of entities with the same attributes (e.g., EMPLOYEE). Shown as a Rectangular Box.
  • Entity Set: The collection of all actual entities at a specific point in time.

Types of Attributes

  • Simple (Atomic): Indivisible (e.g., SSN, Sex).
  • Composite: Divided into subparts (e.g., Address $\to$ Street, City).
  • Multi-valued: Can have multiple values (e.g., Color $\to$ {Red, Black}). Shown as Double Oval.
  • Derived: Calculated from others (e.g., Age from BirthDate). Shown as Dashed Oval.
  • Key: Unique identifier. Underlined in text.

Relationships

A Relationship relates two or more distinct entities. Shown as a Diamond.

Degree of Relationship

  • Unary (Recursive): Entity relates to itself (e.g., Employee supervises Employee).
  • Binary: Two entities (e.g., Employee works for Department). Most common.
  • Ternary: Three entities (e.g., Supplier supplies Part to Project).

Constraints

  • Cardinality Ratio: Max number of relationship instances (1:1, 1:N, M:N).
  • Participation (Existence Dependency):
    - Total (Double Line): Every entity MUST participate.
    - Partial (Single Line): Participation is optional.

Weak Entities

Definition

An entity type that does not have a key attribute of its own.
It must be identified by its relationship to an Owner (Identifying) Entity via an Identifying Relationship.

Notation:
  • Weak Entity: Double Rectangle.
  • Identifying Relationship: Double Diamond.
  • Partial Key: Dashed Underline (e.g., Dependent Name).

The Exam Vault

Professor's Secrets & Trap Avoidance

TRAP: Cardinality Notation

(1,1) vs (0,N):
Many students confuse Min/Max notation.
(Min, Max) = (0, 1) means Participation is Partial (0) and Cardinality is 1.
(Min, Max) = (1, N) means Participation is Total (1) and Cardinality is N.

TRAP: Weak Entity Key

"Does a Weak Entity have a Primary Key?"
Technically No. It has a Partial Key (Discriminator). Its unique identity is formed by (Owner PK + Partial Key).

SECRET: Attribute on Relationship

Attributes don't always belong to Entities.
Example: "Hours Worked". Does it belong to Employee? No. Project? No. It belongs to the WORKS_ON relationship (the intersection of both). This happens in M:N relationships.

KEY CONCEPT: Recursive Relationship

When an entity relates to itself (e.g., "Supervision").
Role Names become critical here (e.g., 1 is "Supervisor", N is "Supervisee") to distinguish the participation of the same entity type.
